# SECTION: NARRATIVE

### The G&N Story

G&N Brands SpA is a 30-year-old Chilean restaurant group with 300+ locations across seven brands, making a calculated entry into the world's largest franchise market. Their US expansion is anchored by two brands &mdash; Tommy Burritos and Chick'n Spot &mdash; each adapted from proven Chilean models (Tommy Beans and Barrio Chick'en) for American palates.

The thesis: the US QSR market rewards operational simplicity and authentic differentiation. Tommy Burritos brings genuine Latin American Tex-Mex heritage to a category dominated by US-invented "Mexican" concepts. Chick'n Spot enters the fastest-growing QSR segment with a streamlined chicken menu designed for franchisee efficiency.

The timing is deliberate. Texas and Florida rank #1 and #2 nationally for franchise growth (IFA 2026). Both are zero-income-tax states with booming populations and deep franchise ecosystems. Texas alone has over 82,000 franchise units, making it the largest franchise state in the country.

The growth vehicle is a master franchise model designed for experienced multi-unit operators. G&N is targeting strategic markets with proven demand, leveraging a scalable framework grounded in 30+ years of multi-brand operations across Latin America.

# SECTION: TOMMY

### Tommy Burritos &mdash; Competitive Landscape

#### About Tommy Burritos

Tommy Burritos is G&N's Tex-Mex franchise concept, adapted from Tommy Beans &mdash; a brand that's been feeding Chileans since 2009 with customizable burritos, tacos, and bowls. The US version keeps the soul of the original: fresh, made-to-order Latin American Tex-Mex with real heritage behind it. That's the differentiator. Every other Tex-Mex chain in the US was invented here. Tommy Burritos was born in Santiago.

Core menu: Burritos, tacos, bowls, quesadillas &mdash; all customizable. Price position: Value-oriented, below Chipotle. Format: In-line ($761K&ndash;$1.02M) or standalone with drive-thru ($1.18M&ndash;$1.52M). Philosophical bet: Authenticity beats invention. A 30-year-old Latin American brand has a story no US competitor can replicate.

The Tex-Mex franchise space splits into two tiers. Taco Bell dominates with 8,244 US locations, while Chipotle (4,051 locations, corporately-owned) proves massive demand. The Tex-Mex restaurant market reached $49.2B globally in 2024 with 6.7% CAGR (compound annual growth rate) projected through 2033. Below the leaders is a fragmented landscape of regional brands. Tommy Burritos enters the mid-market with authentic Chilean Tex-Mex heritage &mdash; a genuine cultural origin story that no US-created competitor can match.

# SECTION: CHICKN

### Chick'n Spot &mdash; Competitive Landscape

#### About Chick'n Spot

Chick'n Spot is G&N's fried chicken franchise concept, adapted from Barrio Chick'en &mdash; launched in Chile in 2021 and already scaling fast. The playbook is simple: do one thing and do it well. Tenders, nuggets, burgers, wraps. Limited SKUs means faster throughput, simpler training, lower food waste, and happier franchisees.

Core menu: Chicken tenders, nuggets, burgers, wraps, salads. Price position: Value-to-mid, between Church's and Chick-fil-A. Format: In-line ($851K&ndash;$1.12M) or standalone ($1.25M&ndash;$1.61M). Philosophical bet: Raising Cane's proved that one protein + menu simplicity = explosive growth. Chick'n Spot is built on the same principle.

The chicken QSR category generates $63.7B in annual US revenue with 5.6% CAGR since 2020. Chicken now accounts for 37% of QSR food spend, up 2 points in two years. Chick-fil-A dominates at ~45% share with industry-leading $7.5M average unit volumes. Raising Cane's has surged past KFC to ~11% share on the strength of menu simplicity &mdash; precisely the model Chick'n Spot is built on. ##### What This Means for G&N

What your competitors are doing right now &mdash; and what it means for G&N's playbook. These aren't abstract trends; they're specific moves by specific brands that will shape the TX/FL competitive landscape over the next 3&ndash;5 years.

Chipotle is building 350+ new locations with digital-first layouts and Chipotlane drive-thru as the default format. Wingstop deployed its "Smart Kitchen" to nearly all US restaurants, delivering mid-single-digit comp lift. Starbucks is closing ~400 locations and remodeling 1,000+ stores as part of a $1B suburban pivot. The message: the future of QSR is smaller, faster, and more efficient.

The global ghost kitchen market hit $88.4B in 2025 and is projected to reach $196.7B by 2032 (12.1% CAGR). Wingstop, Ruby Tuesday, TGI Fridays are all operating ghost kitchen concepts. McDonald's is testing a delivery-only restaurant in London. These aren't experiments anymore &mdash; they're permanent operational models. G&N could test delivery-only concepts before committing to full buildouts in new markets.

The industry is in a full-blown value war. McDonald's launched a $3 value menu in April 2026. Taco Bell rolled out its Luxe Value Menu &mdash; 10 items all under $3, plus tiered Cravings Boxes at $5/$7/$9. Popeyes brought back its $5 Faves Menu. Wholesale food prices are up 4.3% vs. 2024 (beef, bread, dairy hit hardest), squeezing margins. Lower-income diners are eating out less.

Both Tommy Burritos and Chick'n Spot are positioned as value brands &mdash; which is exactly where the consumer is going. But value pricing compresses margins. G&N's advantage: entering with optimized COGS from day one (34% target), no legacy menu bloat, and a streamlined SKU count that keeps food waste low. The brands that win the value war won't be the cheapest &mdash; they'll be the most operationally efficient.

McDonald's is deploying AI-driven automation across ~43K restaurants &mdash; predictive cooking, voice ordering, equipment monitoring. SoundHound voice AI is integrating with POS systems for automated drive-thru order-taking. 2026 is being called "the year of the AI-driven restaurant."

Sweetgreen's Infinite Kitchen delivers 700 bps labor savings, peaks at 500 orders/hour. Chipotle's Chippy robot (via Miso Robotics) handles chip production with AI-precise seasoning. Kitchen automation is moving from novelty to necessity.

62% of QSR brands are using or adding kiosks, with ~80% adoption rate industry-wide. Over 70% of orders are now placed via mobile. 56% of consumers prefer third-party apps. Self-service isn't the future &mdash; it's the present.

G&N enters with zero tech debt. No legacy POS to replace, no ancient ordering system to work around. The playbook: launch with modern cloud POS, mobile-first ordering, and kiosk-ready layouts from location #1. AI-driven demand forecasting and kitchen management should be in the Year 2 roadmap. The brands that resist technology will be the ones that lose the next decade.

Chipotle made plant-based chorizo a permanent menu item. Taco Bell launched a vegan nacho cheese sauce (cashew-based) nationwide. Panda Express made Beyond Orange Chicken permanent. The flexitarian trend is real: brands blending meat with mushrooms/soy for lower calories and better margins. Tommy Burritos' customizable format naturally supports plant-based additions.

The chicken category hit $63.7B with 5.6% CAGR. Raising Cane's (+1.6pts share) and Wingstop both surpassed KFC. Chicken now accounts for 37% of QSR food spend. Consumers view chicken as the healthier protein. The Tex-Mex market reached $49.2B globally (6.7% CAGR to 2033). Both categories are growing &mdash; G&N picked the right lanes.

Global online food delivery: $242.7B in 2026, projected $540.6B by 2035 (9.3% CAGR). 60% of consumers prefer contactless delivery. Chipotle generates 36% of revenue from digital channels. 3P delivery commissions (15&ndash;30%) are squeezing margins &mdash; smart chains are building first-party channels to capture data and reduce fees.

Multi-unit franchisees represent 19.3% of operators but control 58.8% of all locations (IFA 2026). Average initial franchise investment: $1.81M&ndash;$4.94M (down 2.7% YoY). Both G&N brands sit below this average &mdash; accessible entry points for operators diversifying their portfolios. The market rewards scale, and G&N's master franchise model is built for it.

Chick'n Spot has a protein cost advantage heading into 2026 &mdash; chicken production is expanding while beef is constrained. Tommy Burritos should plan for elevated beef/pork costs and consider menu engineering (chicken protein options, plant-forward items) to protect margins.

Key insight: Menu simplicity + brand trust are winning. Raising Cane's and Chick-fil-A dominate with focused menus. Chipotle's growth is slowing due to higher prices. Value dining is outperforming hype.

The industry is in a paradox: consumers love restaurants (87%) but are cutting frequency (40%+ doing less). The winners are brands with strong value propositions and loyalty programs &mdash; 81% of consumers prioritize deals when choosing where to eat. G&N's Year 1 royalty discount (3%) gives franchisees margin room to compete on value, and building a loyalty program from day one should be a launch priority, not a Phase 2 initiative.

##### Caveat

How do competing QSR brands recruit, screen, and incentivize franchisees in 2026? This section maps the current posture of every major Tex-Mex and chicken competitor — whether they're open, closed, or selectively hunting multi-unit operators, plus active incentives and recent strategic moves. Useful context for positioning Tommy Burritos and Chick'n Spot against the pack when pitching master franchisees.

(1) Multi-unit is the default. Most chains now require 3+ unit development commitments ($500K&ndash;$1.5M net worth typical). Single-unit applicants are increasingly unwelcome. (2) Several major US brands are closed. Chipotle never franchised; Raising Cane's ended their short-lived program; Five Guys is sold out in the US and Canada (international only). (3) Veterans, women, and minority programs are active. Golden Chick offers a fee discount for veterans; Bojangles actively courts women/minority-owned groups; Culver's runs a manager-to-owner mentorship pipeline.

Three of the most coveted chicken brands are off the table for operators: Raising Cane's (corporate-only), Five Guys (US sold out), and Chick-fil-A (operator model, not ownership). Multi-unit operators looking for a chicken concept they can actually own and scale have a narrower field than headlines suggest. Chick'n Spot fits that gap.

Chipotle — the #2 Tex-Mex concept by unit count and #1 by cultural relevance — has never franchised and never will. Operators who want authentic, fresh-prepared Latin concepts have no Chipotle-caliber option. Tommy Burritos' authentic Chilean heritage is the closest narrative match available to franchisees.

Qdoba's $100K cash incentive for 3-unit commitments by Sep 2026 is the most aggressive recruitment offer in the Tex-Mex space. G&N should consider a similar time-boxed launch incentive — tied to master franchise signing in TX or FL — to accelerate the first cohort and generate market-making announcements.

Golden Chick (33% veteran fee discount) and Bojangles (women/minority focus) show this is increasingly table-stakes. G&N can differentiate by explicitly packaging a "First-Generation Operator" or "Latin-Owned Franchisee" program — leaning into the Chilean heritage while signaling genuine community access.

Both Slim Chickens and Huey Magoo's launched 1,500-sq-ft prototypes in 2025&ndash;26 to lower buildout costs and shorten ramp. This directly improves franchisee unit economics and is a selling point in every discovery day. Chick'n Spot's streamlined SKU set is a natural fit for a compact-box version.

Del Taco is aggressively signing FL deals (44+ units planned, Panhandle + SE FL). Culver's FL growth is the largest YoY of any state (119→126). Church's is hiring operators in GA/SC/NC/VA — adjacent markets. Tommy Burritos and Chick'n Spot need to move quickly on TX/FL master franchise signings before the prime multi-unit operators are contractually locked up.

Recruiting posture can shift quickly — leadership changes, PE acquisitions, or market saturation announcements can all open or close a brand's program within a quarter.

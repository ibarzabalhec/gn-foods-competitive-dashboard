## 1. Executive Summary: Why This Dashboard Matters

- **One source of truth for a multi-dimensional decision.** Investors evaluating a U.S. QSR franchise normally have to triangulate IFA reports, BLS labor data, ScrapeHero unit counts, NRN rankings, state regulatory filings, and brand-specific decks before they can even decide whether to take a second meeting. The G&N dashboard collapses all of that into a single cross-referenced view organized by the actual decision sequence: market → brands → competitors → economics → operations → site selection.

- **Removes the "first-time entrant" discount.** Latin American operators expanding into the U.S. are routinely valued below U.S.-born concepts because investors assume the operator has not done the local work. A dashboard with this depth — 19 fully sourced sections, web-verified competitor benchmarks placed next to G&N's own unit economics — signals that G&N has already done the diligence the investor would otherwise have to do themselves.

- **Pre-empts the standard objection sequence.** When a multi-unit operator hears "Chilean brand entering Texas," the next eight questions are predictable: Why these states? Why these categories? Who already operates here? What is the build-out cost vs. competitors? Is there a Chick-fil-A nearby? Each of those questions has its own dashboard section with the answer already drawn out and sourced.

- **The dashboard is itself a sales artifact.** The same buyer who sees a polished, data-driven self-service intelligence platform will assume the operations behind it are equally rigorous. The dashboard becomes proof of how G&N will run the franchise system — not just a description of it.

- **Scales the pitch across investor types.** A single tool serves the single-unit franchisee evaluating Tommy Burritos in San Antonio, the multi-unit operator considering five Chick'n Spot locations across Houston, and the PE-backed platform exploring a Texas master franchise. Each can navigate to the sections they care about and skip the rest.

- **Makes G&N look investor-ready before the first call.** The asset signals that G&N is approaching the U.S. with the same operational discipline that built 300+ locations and 7 brands across 30 years in Chile — and that signal alone shortens the diligence timeline.

## 2. How the Dashboard Supports G&N's Investor Pitch

The dashboard is structured to walk an investor through every gate they will hit between "interested" and "signing the master franchise agreement." Each gate is mapped to evidence already on the page.

**Who is G&N and why does its track record matter?**
The Hero and Narrative sections lead with concrete operating credentials: G&N Brands SpA, 30+ years, 300+ locations across seven concepts in Chile, U.S. headquarters in Miami on Brickell. This is not a startup pitching a deck — it is a multi-brand operator that has already proven it can build, run, and scale restaurant systems. For a franchisee, that translates into one specific question: *Will this franchisor still be here in 10 years?* The track record answers it.

**Why Tommy Burritos and Chick'n Spot specifically?**
The brand sections position each concept against the lane it is entering. Tommy Burritos is framed against the Chipotle / Taco Bell / Qdoba landscape with a single differentiator that is both legally defensible and commercially scarce: it is the only major U.S.-market Tex-Mex concept with an authentic Latin American origin (Tommy Beans, Chile, since 2009). Chick'n Spot is framed against the Raising Cane's playbook — proof that a streamlined chicken menu can scale into the thousands of units — with the financial model already costed for U.S. unit economics.

**Why Texas and Florida are the right Phase 1 markets.**
The Market and Demographics sections answer this question with verifiable data: Texas is #1 nationally for franchise growth (2.6% unit expansion, 82,000+ franchise units), Florida is #2, both are zero-income-tax states, and both have Hispanic populations that align disproportionately with Tex-Mex authenticity (Texas 40%, San Antonio 65%, Florida 26%). The Site Selection section translates that demographic data into a metro-by-metro launch ranking.

**How each brand compares against the actual competitive set.**
The competitive landscape tables for both brands are not abstract. They are unit-by-unit benchmarks against the brands the investor will already know — Chipotle, Taco Bell, Raising Cane's, Chick-fil-A, Wingstop, Popeyes, Bojangles, Dave's Hot Chicken, Slim Chickens. Investment ranges, royalty structures, AUVs, store counts, digital maturity, and recruiting posture are presented in a single grid for direct comparison.

**What the franchise economics look like at the unit level.**
The Economics section provides full P&L modeling for both in-line and standalone formats: Year 1 projections, royalty structure (5% with a Year 1 discount), labor and food cost targets, and the path to break-even. The What-If Scenario Calculator lets an operator stress-test the model against their own assumptions in real time.

**What capital is required.**
Tommy Burritos: $40K franchise fee, $761K–$1.02M in-line investment, $1.18M–$1.52M standalone with drive-thru. Chick'n Spot: $40K fee, $851K–$1.12M in-line, $1.25M–$1.61M standalone. Both sit below the IFA's 2026 average initial franchise investment ($1.81M–$4.94M) — a deliberate accessibility signal designed for multi-unit operators diversifying their portfolios.

**Market, demographic, labor, regulatory, and real estate context.**
Dedicated sections cover each: Texas labor floor ($7.25 vs. Florida $14+), state-by-state franchise registration requirements, suburban vs. urban real estate cost differentials (40–60% spread), and the right-to-work status of both states. Each is presented as an input to unit-level cost modeling rather than as background reading.

**Which metros are most attractive for launch.**
The Site Selection section ranks metros for each brand: San Antonio first for Tommy Burritos (cultural fit, affordable real estate), Houston for Chick'n Spot volume, Tampa for the Florida beachhead (best real estate value in the state), Austin and Orlando for Gen Z–adjacent university plays.

**Which risks remain and what diligence questions to ask.**
The dashboard does not pretend the bet is risk-free. The Trends section surfaces the value-war pressure on QSR margins, the 6-in-10 operators reporting traffic declines in 2025, the wage-cost trajectory, and the third-party delivery commission squeeze. The SWOT section synthesizes the full picture into per-brand strategic frameworks. This honesty is itself a credibility signal: investors trust operators who show them the risks more than operators who hide them.

## 3. Investor Decision-Making Value

The dashboard is built to answer, on a single screen, the ten questions every multi-unit operator and master franchise candidate asks before committing capital.

**What am I investing in?**
A U.S. adaptation of two proven Chilean restaurant systems — Tommy Beans (Tex-Mex, since 2009) becoming Tommy Burritos, and Barrio Chick'en (chicken, since 2021) becoming Chick'n Spot — operated under a master franchise structure built for experienced U.S. operators.

**Why now?**
Texas and Florida lead U.S. franchise growth for 2026 (IFA, verified). Chicken QSR is the fastest-growing protein category at 5.6% CAGR with chicken now at 37% of QSR food spend. Tex-Mex globally hit $49.2B with 6.7% CAGR projected through 2033. Both lanes are expanding while a value war is squeezing legacy brands — exactly the window for a fresh, low-COGS entrant with an authenticity story.

**Why these brands?**
Tommy Burritos owns a competitive position no U.S. Tex-Mex brand can replicate (authentic Latin American origin) at a price point below Chipotle. Chick'n Spot enters with a Raising Cane's-style menu discipline (focused SKUs, fast service) at a sub-$1.2M in-line investment.

**Why these markets?**
Texas and Florida = #1 and #2 in U.S. franchise growth, zero income tax, deep multi-unit operator ecosystems, and demographic skews (young, growing, Hispanic-heavy in Texas) that align with both brand propositions.

**How much capital is required?**
$761K–$1.61M per unit depending on brand and format. Master franchise territory rights are priced separately, based on market potential and expected unit density.

**What are the potential returns?**
The What-If Calculator gives the investor full control over assumptions. The Economics section provides G&N's projections, anchored to comparable-brand AUVs (Chick-fil-A $7.5M, Raising Cane's, Chipotle benchmarks) so the investor can sanity-check the model against the actual U.S. operating environment.

**Who are the competitors?**
Tommy Burritos: Taco Bell (8,244 U.S. units), Chipotle (4,051, corporate-only), Qdoba, Del Taco, regional Tex-Mex concepts. Chick'n Spot: Chick-fil-A, Raising Cane's, KFC, Popeyes, Wingstop, Dave's Hot Chicken, Bojangles, Slim Chickens. Each profiled with current U.S. unit count, expansion posture, fee structure, and digital maturity.

**What risks should I evaluate?**
Industry-wide traffic decline (6-in-10 operators in 2025), wage inflation (Florida side), third-party delivery commission compression, and brand-recognition gap (G&N has zero U.S. consumer awareness on day one). All surfaced explicitly in the Trends and SWOT sections.

**What would the rollout strategy look like?**
The Site Selection section provides a phased, brand-specific launch sequence: Tommy Burritos in San Antonio first; Chick'n Spot in Houston first; Florida beachhead via Tampa; university-adjacent secondary markets in Austin and Orlando. Each metro choice is justified by the underlying demographic, real estate, and competitive density data on the page.

**What should I ask G&N before committing?**
The dashboard implicitly arms the investor with the right questions: validation of AUV projections against comparable U.S. units, FDD review, territory protection terms, marketing fund structure, supply chain in TX/FL, and ramp expectations between Year 1 and Year 3. By making these questions visible rather than hiding them, G&N controls the diligence narrative.

## 4. Dashboard as a Handover Tool for Investors

After a discovery call or pitch meeting, most investors want to *go look at the materials again* before committing to a follow-up. The standard QSR pitch pack — a slide deck, an FDD, a one-pager — is static, fragmented, and forgettable. The dashboard changes that posture.

**A self-guided diligence portal.** An investor who got 30 minutes of conversation on the brand can spend three hours on the dashboard exploring whatever questions came up afterward — without G&N having to schedule another call. The conversation continues in their own time, on their own depth, in the parts of the model that matter most to them.

**A one-stop shop.** Brand story, market research, competitive intelligence, franchise economics, demographics, site selection, regulatory landscape, recruiting strategy, and strategic synthesis all live at one URL. The investor does not have to chase down separate decks or PDFs to triangulate the picture.

**Supplement, not replacement, for legal disclosures.** The FDD is the binding document. The dashboard is the sales and education layer that sits in front of it — and points to it. This positioning matters legally: the dashboard educates and qualifies, but does not promise specific returns or substitute for the disclosure framework. Used correctly, it makes the FDD easier to open and easier to take seriously.

**An embedding surface for official assets.** The dashboard is the natural home for downloadable Tommy Burritos and Chick'n Spot one-pagers, FDD references where legally appropriate, brand application links, executive team bios, the official corporate site, and contact forms. Everything an investor needs to move forward sits in one place.

**A tool that keeps the conversation warm.** Every time a multi-unit operator forwards the dashboard URL to their CFO, their attorney, or their investment committee, G&N's brand and pitch travel with it — in the operator's words, on the operator's timing. This is the difference between a one-meeting pitch and a deal that compounds across an organization.

## 5. Recommended Dashboard Improvements Before G&N Uses It Externally

Prioritized — most critical first.

### Tier 1 — Block external send until done

1. **Resolve consistency between dashboard figures and the FDD.** Investors will scrutinize every number. Any drift between the dashboard and the binding document is a credibility-ending error. One full sweep against the FDD — franchise fees, royalty structure, projected economics, unit counts — is mandatory before publishing externally.

2. **Add legal disclaimers throughout.** "Projections are illustrative, not guaranteed returns." "Not an offer to sell a franchise." "FDD is the binding document — please review before any commitment." Standard franchise-industry boilerplate, but legally required for external use.

3. **Add official G&N branding.** Logo, brand colors, footer with corporate info, copyright, and consistent typography matching G&N's existing brand book. The current build is functional but generic — it does not yet visually signal that this is a G&N asset.

4. **Publish under a G&N-owned domain.** Something like `invest.gnbrands.com`, `franchise.tommyburritos.com`, or `gnfranchise.com`. Sending investors to a generic file URL undermines every other credibility signal we are trying to establish.

### Tier 2 — Strongly recommended before first investor send

5. **Embed downloadable PDFs.** Tommy Burritos one-pager, Chick'n Spot one-pager, FDD reference (or summary), executive team bio sheet, market summary by city. Investors expect to download and forward — make that frictionless.

6. **Add a contact / next-step form.** A real conversion mechanism: "Request a discovery call," "Download the FDD," "Speak to an area developer." Tracked, with CRM integration if possible. Without this, the dashboard is a dead end after the investor finishes reading.

7. **Add investor segmentation.** A landing path that asks: *Are you a single-unit operator, multi-unit operator, area developer, master franchise candidate, or PE-backed platform?* — and routes them to the relevant subset of the dashboard. Different audiences need different anchors and different next steps.

8. **Improve Spanish translation quality to investor-grade.** For a Chilean parent company pitching to Latin American capital and to Hispanic operators in TX/FL, the bilingual asset is a credibility marker, not a nice-to-have. The v2 audit work in progress addresses this — make sure it is finalized and externally reviewed before launch.

### Tier 3 — Polish that increases conversion

9. **Add a "How to Use This Dashboard" section.** A 30-second orientation for first-time visitors. Sets expectations and routes them to the section most relevant to their role and stage.

10. **Add downloadable per-city market summaries.** Investors who self-identify a metro should be able to download a one-page brief on it. This makes the dashboard portable inside their organization.

11. **Versioning and date stamping.** Every section should carry a "last updated" timestamp. Industry data goes stale — investors who see a stale date discount the entire document.

12. **Analytics and engagement tracking.** G&N should know which investors visited, which sections they spent time on, and what they downloaded. This feeds the recruiting team's follow-up timing and personalization.
